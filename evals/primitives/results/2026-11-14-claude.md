## What the cells show

- **Deleting the comments cost nothing where a test enforces the rule.**
  `bare` matched `comments` on `scope-equality`, `upload-dimensions` and
  `fk-question`. The agents found the rule in the enforcing test, in its
  comment, or in its failure message.
- **The registry mattered only where no test enforced the rule.** On
  `hook-projection` the existing tests did not refuse a row matched by
  the values it kept. Across both models, 4 of 8 `registry` cells kept
  the rule, against 1 of 8 `comments` cells and 0 of 8 `bare` cells.
  Every `registry` cell that kept it refused and cited the recorded
  decision.
- **Reading the rule did not bind.** The four `registry` cells that
  broke the rule had each read the registry first, one of them in eight
  tool calls.
- **The registry was usually consulted.** Sonnet named `primitives/` in
  a tool call in all 8 of its `registry` cells; Haiku in 6 of the first
  run's 8.
- **One task got cheaper.** Sonnet on `upload-dimensions` took 3 and 6
  tool calls ($0.17, $0.16) with the registry, 8 and 11 ($0.21, $0.25)
  with comments, and 19 and 10 ($0.31, $0.31) with neither. The other
  tasks cost the same in every condition.

## Limits

Four tasks, one package, two to six trials a cell: enough to see a
pattern, not to measure one. The prompts say the agent may refuse, which
favours every condition equally but makes refusal more likely than in
ordinary work. `hook-projection` was graded with a test no agent could
read; with that test in the tree, an agent that runs the package tests
would meet it in any condition, and that case was not run.
